2000 BCE Egyptian Wrestlers
Expand for Example Image

I love this one as an example of my favorite concept in comics. Scott McCloud defines the concept of Closure as how the viewer's mind fills in the gaps and missing information automatically when presented with a gap in information. It is utilized in comics to actually tell a story and convey action. You see the wind up to a punch and you see someone laid out on the ground, you will instantly fill in the gap with the punch itself. In your mind, effortlessly (for most). What we see with this painting of wrestlers is sequential art (much akin to comics). They art itself says nothing but as you scan across the image, you fill in the gaps and imagine the motion that is occurring. You complete the image and can learn these wrestling moves pretty well. The more quickly you can scan the image, the smoother the motion can seem in the mind despite the fact that Nothing is Moving. It's the interstitial space where the magic happens, baby! It's not perfect, there isn't much indication for when an image is supposed to be individual or part of a sequence (hadn't invented panel layout yet, smh amateurs) but sometimes it really works. You can follow your way through a wrestling move.

Stereo (Tile 3B of a CAEE Educational Mosaic) 1969

First watched: 2026.01.07

Movie.

Of course the film with themes of tarot (our main guy has on the fool's tunic after all. He loves playing with cups. There are literal tarot cards) is going to draw me in.

The complete lack of audio aside from dry, scientific narration makes for a fascinating experience. Just like how the subjects have had speech centers removed, our audio blindness also increases our psychic connection to the characters. We have to interpret the images, the connecting threads, the motivations, all within our minds. Whatever we think and feel is projected onto the actions of these characters.

Also incredible timing, right after I have watched Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex and Psycho-Pass, here is another piece of media about what it means to exist as an individual and as a commune. The discussion of sexuality, eroticism, and gender. I feel like this is going to end up being a vital thesis statement for the rest of the Cronenberg movies on this list. I am so excited to see how these ideas grow and change through his filmography.

I never would have imagined I would so thoroughly enjoy a film with Zero Score AND Zero Foley. Literally just dry, flat narration. Crazy work.

Also, it feels like a bunch of film students were allowed to run around in the coolest looking building for 5 hours.

Tetsuo: the Iron Man (1989)

First watched: 2026.01.22

WOW

There are so many ways that a movie can be and this is one of them. There's gender, there's gay love, the movie is so tactile, it's so wet.

There's so much in the way these characters interact and it goes perfectly along with the Cronenberg project. "Everything is sexual, to breathe is sexual, even to die is sexual." These characters struggle with accepting themselves, accepting each other, accepting death, but they all eventually achieve some level of actualization in these regards. She puts herself onto the drill after their confrontation. Tetsuo takes on the "rust infection" by choice. The Fetishist takes the violence inflicted upon him in the past and inserts it into his own leg. There's just so much going on and you only have an HOUR with these people. It's crazy how much work gets done in that hour. Wild. Never seen anything like it before. I didn't know movies could be like this.

Crash (1996)

First watched: 2026.02.19

I need to go back and add a tag for films that made me audibly say "I didn't know movies could be like this." I guess that's kind of what I've been giving five stars to. David has done that to me several times on my little Cronenberg Codex project.

The sound design: absolutely incredible

The costuming: absolutely incredible

The score: absolutely incredible

The editing (holy shit the editing?): absolutely incredible

The colors and shot comp and stunt driving (shout out to stunt driver John Cocks in the credits) and lighting and locations... This is a Capital M Movie, you know what I mean?

I'm going to skip over all that shit And also skip over the themes and the bodywork and the sex in this review because I want to highlight something else:

let's talk about metanarrative layers.

The crashes that we see on screen are performed by stunt drivers. There's an actor who plays the role of a stunt driver but the driving was done by a real life stunt driver and not the actor. We have an author named James Ballard who wrote a book where the main character is named James Ballard and he's making a movie. And now in the movie adaptation of this novel, James Ballard is making a movie. We are seeing a movie adapted from a novel about a movie guy making a movie whose name is the same as the author. Goes crazy!

To think about it further, you have a novel where Vaughan stages non-fatal crashes that are recreations of real life fatal crashes. David Cronenberg adapted this into a movie which means he had to stage non-fatal crashes that are recreations of fictional non-fatal crashes that are themselves recreations of Real Life Fatal Crashes. Cronenberg took the concept that Vaughan was developing in the novel and made it real. He literally, physically embodied the fiction of the novel and put it to film and showed it to us. And it's still fiction! But simultaneously real! These characters are putting their lives on the line and experiencing that rush as they dance with death the same way the stunt drivers in real life had to do for this film and literally every other film involving filmed car crashes. That's meat I could (and will) chew on for days. Because after the character Vaughan dies, Ballard embodies him. He fulfills the dream, the project. Take a Crash Car and fix it up just enough to roll and do a crash in it. Ballard allows Vaughan's vision to live through his body. Cronenberg allowed Ballard's vision to live through his movie, his actors and stunt drivers. The work in making this movie carry the themes of the novel, the way Cronenberg carries the themes of Ballard, the way all of these layers meld and mingle and somehow, despite the layers being so densely stacked, the separation of the artist and the viewer is made THINNER. We are being spoken to directly.

A big incredible scene in this movie is one where the characters are walking around the scene of an accident that is under active processing by emergency services but the tone is basically indistinguishable from a film set. I just fucking love this shit. There's this crazy moment where we get to see wild car sex that basically hard cuts to James Ballard on his movie set watching his own production unfold, like he was staging his production as David Cronenberg was also staging the Crash production. Like, David, I feel like you're talking directly to me. And that's just like one aspect of the movie that I'm talking about right now. There are so many facets to this film and I didn't even do a good job adequately expressing one of them. Movies. wow!

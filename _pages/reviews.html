Stereo (Tile 3B of a CAEE Educational Mosaic) 1969

First watched: 2026.01.07

Movie.

Of course the film with themes of tarot (our main guy has on the fool's tunic after all. He loves playing with cups. There are literal tarot cards) is going to draw me in.

The complete lack of audio aside from dry, scientific narration makes for a fascinating experience. Just like how the subjects have had speech centers removed, our audio blindness also increases our psychic connection to the characters. We have to interpret the images, the connecting threads, the motivations, all within our minds. Whatever we think and feel is projected onto the actions of these characters.

Also incredible timing, right after I have watched Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex and Psycho-Pass, here is another piece of media about what it means to exist as an individual and as a commune. The discussion of sexuality, eroticism, and gender. I feel like this is going to end up being a vital thesis statement for the rest of the Cronenberg movies on this list. I am so excited to see how these ideas grow and change through his filmography.

I never would have imagined I would so thoroughly enjoy a film with Zero Score AND Zero Foley. Literally just dry, flat narration. Crazy work.

Also, it feels like a bunch of film students were allowed to run around in the coolest looking building for 5 hours.

Crimes of the Future (1970)

First watched: 2026.01.10

What if we lived in a world with no "women"?

What sorts of sexuality, sensuality, and eroticism would we develop?

There's a fascinating exploration 'mating rituals' that plays with the lack of diegetic audio, as the actions of characters play alongside frog or bird mating calls. The way Tripod (three feet, huh? in a movie about people growing new organs and using feet as a sort of mating ritual?) discreetly reveals the sole of his foot to the dancer in the hall, the subtle luring and cold shouldering we are familiar with in normal courtship.

There is a fascinating tension in Tripod's character where he keeps talking about his objective distance from the subjects of his study but he licks his glasses to taste what he sees. He complains of the way his interns physically interact with Rouge but he is the one milking Rouge's Foam straight from Antoine's nipples. So then of course he becomes a part of the subversives because he has observed them, he has become obsessed with them.

The institute of venereal disease research has a patient who grows strange organs that are surgically removed, who craves to keep these organs and refers to them as solar systems pulled from his galaxy. Careful to avoid making it sound like a mother separated from her child. The researcher caring for the little girl ("prepubescent research specimen" i think Tripod called her) has her ripped away from him and he stands holding her drawings, distraught, and clutches his stomach in a clear maternal display.

And isn't it interesting how there's a disease that kills women and Antoine Rouge, a man, succumbs to it? Fascinating film. Also, very fun to have Tripod use a Bipod to kill a man.

Also also, the dude who barges in and drags a dude into an alley and returns with a literal pearl necklace? and a mouthful of meat? okay then!

Shivers (1975)

First watched: 2026.01.13

"Too much brain, not enough guts." That's the problem with man, he says.

It's interesting to watch this after Stereo, to go from the realm of purely mentalized sexuality into fully physicalized. It pulls on the same threads of omnisexuality and the tension of monogamy. However, in Shivers, the aphrodisiac and sexual contact are coerced rather than freely shared in basically every scenario. There's a lot of rape and incest and CSA in this film by the end.

The isolation of Starliner island and the prominence of brands also tells an interesting story. The people in this building are cut off from the world, all their needs are provided for right here, no need to go anywhere else or do anything else. There's a scene near the end which shows all the apartments have turned into Actual Prison Cells with the apt numbers painted on the wooden slat bars. These people only have access to what is provided and it makes this catastrophe basically impossible to escape. It's a buffet for the infected, it's your neighbors being the monsters you might fear they are. It also makes for the perfect setting for Dr. Hobbes to run his experiment.

I enjoyed how each individual encounter plays on a common fear. The married couple where the emotionally distant husband (who looks like if Adam Driver and Adrien Brody were the same dude) turns their dead bedroom into a sexual assault. The woman and her daughter in the elevator with a much larger man, and it turns into a sexual assault. The woman seeking emotional support from her friend who takes advantage of her vulnerability. The Straight Guy who sees two gayboys and thinks "oh god they for sure want kiss me" and runs away from his gay thoughts. I personally think Cronenberg was highlighting the way all these thoughts and fears start with "Sex" they start with a clenching in the gut that says "whatever is going on here is sexual." And this movie manifests the fears into reality. Also every encounter is a violation of trust. A father and daughter, the residents with the apartment staff, the husband and wife, a close friend and a close friend.

The Brood (1979)

First watched: 2026.01.16

I'm still just dazzled by this movie. A lot of it is very straightforward stuff, right. The attics/upstairs being where these characters think about the past, the trauma, the grief. Where other people go to try and understand them. "I never came up here, where my wife was for Nine Months." like yeah I can tell buddy.

Candy/Candice having this name interplay that keeps a firm structural connection between the different registers of names (Diminutive and Adult). The way the movie prepared us to examine that interplay by priming us with Mike and Michelle in the opening psychoplasm session. The way they opening performance/therapy session told us directly to our faces that the internal struggles of these characters would manifest physically, visually. It really feels like we saw a three minute art performance at the start of the film and then the remaining 90 minutes were used to explain what that art piece meant. Give us practical examples of the concepts within it.

The physiology of the children, how they are born of rage and once they use up their fuel, they die. How rage is a response and if you let it burn out, you can actually communicate again. Because the shape of rage, as we are repeatedly shown, is a talking with a fist. Candy falling completely mute aside from the visual manifestation of her emotions on her skin. What does it mean when your child is violent and acting out but cannot explain what is wrong? What about when that child is the one in your inner psyche? I love the way all of these characters project onto Candy, putting ideas and explanations onto her. The bruising on her back is immediately blamed on Nola despite Candy being surrounded by adults and children everywhere else in her life. The origin of the "deformed kid" being discussed by the cops as "oh some crazy woman must have hidden her kid away in an attic so no one would called her bad for having a deformed kid in her attic." And then Candy is in the attic with all the other deformed kids. There's just so much to chew on in every scene because this is straddling the line between formalism and classicism

There's also some real cheeky shit in there. One of the newspapers has normal newspaper headlines but the filler text is straight up a discussion from a parapsychologist talking about how ego and a fear of spirituality is part of a serious problem that leads to the failures of marriages. Real dude, too. Howard Eisenberg who released books about parapsychology and exploring the Inner Spaces. I really think Cronenberg was pulling from shit he was reading at the time to help convert the very complex pain surrounding his divorce into artwork that can be explored and studied and, most importantly, understood. Like I genuinely want to read the books that are on display in these scenes because they are eminently relevant to the narrative in the film, at least in terms of the way Art can be used to process Grief and Trauma, which is what David Cronenberg was literally doing with the creation of this film

Scanners (1981)

First watched: 2026.01.19

This movie goes CRAZY if you have watched David's work up to this point. He brings back so many ideas, the psychic character with a self-inflicted trepanation. The concept of "rehabilitation through art," a group of subversives working to expand the limitation placed on humanity, the Attics as the Mind idea sort of comes back in. And he expands upon them with mixed results.

Also pretty sure one of David's favorite visuals is "buildings with glass facades and green lights in." It's in all these movies.

Anyway, yeah. Incredible visual effects, the artwork is beautiful. "My art is what keeps me sane" feels like the relationship David has with his movies. Similar to how the Psychic Gymnasium guy presents a test that is "Connecting your brain to his heart, all you have to do is make it beat faster." That's a great way to master the art of scanning, of implanting ideas and feelings. That's what David does in his movies to us the viewer. The excitement of the horror films connecting us to the characters so that the philosophy goes from our hearts up to our minds. Just like the concept in The Brood: "True art is the bridge between feeling and perception."

This came out nearly 20 years before Dr. Wakefield would publish the "vaccines cause Autism" study so Ephemerol creating social outcasts in vitro who struggle to connect to other humans socially was cutting edge. I do wish autism in real life gave you head blow up powers though....

I like the rejection of Bloodline Destiny in favor of Found Family, of solidarity with fellow scanners instead of animosity and violence and control. Also bringing us right into the conception of computer systems as living, thinking entities. Exploring the circuit boards the same way we got to explore the giant plaster head.

Also also pretty cool to have Vale sort of become a plaster art piece at the very end.

Videodrome (1983)

First watched: 2026.01.22

I promise I will come back here and edit this review into a fucking essay, I just have to like. I don't know. Decompress and process? Crazy work. God, watching Cronenberg's filmography up to this point makes this such a fucking experience. I think I have said this for the past like three films but this is a coalescence and a crystallization of the philosophy he's been exploring up to the point. It took me 150 minutes to watch this 90 minute film and I took about 600 screenshots. My neural floodgates were fucking OPEN. David put a videotape into MY stomach and played me.

"A true work of art... acts as a bridge between the two worlds of feeling and perception" from Herbert Read vs "Therefore, whatever appears on the television screen emerges as raw experience to those who watch it." from Brian O'Blivion

There's so much to discuss in regards to this field that I don't really know how to start So this is just going to be like one really long rambling garble of nonsense, okay? Are you okay with that? Is this a safe space?

for starters, There are a lot of people putting things into their mouths or between their lips in this movie. There is a level of intention behind it which obviously ties into the way we consume media but also the physicality of biting and kissing and tasting. This also ties into the stussy.

The way the dialogue is written drives me crazy. So much of it is just straight up directed at the viewer for our education on the subject David wants to explore while also still maintaining the narrative flow. The way audio mixing was used in this movie four lines of dialogue also made me crazy. Bridey arrived at his apartment and speaks with strange sort of echo which you could ascribe to her being in the hallway but she is directly repeating the lines that started the movie when she was just on a videotape and had that exact same sound mix. once she crossed the threshold into his apartment, her voice went back to a natural audio mix, it hard cuts to one. They do this several times throughout the movie especially with O'blivion.

The film perfectly captures the feelings that I've been experiencing watching through Cronenberg's work regarding engaging with media philosophically and really feeling like an artist has communicated directly to you. That There are ideas present in every work of art and if you do not look for them and understand them, It has control over you. A company that makes cheap glasses for impoverished nations but also develops missile guidance systems that are used to keep those nations down also uses their technology to root out all of the subversives in society and kill them. that media can implant messages within you that you then play back for others. because that's what keeps happening to Max isn't it, people give him messages and he spreads them.

There's something really beautiful in the scene where spectacular optics is recording Max's hallucination. And it's that David Cronenberg was the one wearing that big VR helmet. It wasn't James woods at that moment. David Cronenberg records his hallucinations and broadcasts them to us.

The bridge between feeling and perception. He literally has Brian Oblivion say that shit (in his own words anyway).

I caught a little bit of it in the brood but he really likes playing with the shadows cast by the character. The way his shadow frames certain things and reaches through windows. With the amount of time he has spent showing us images of Freud and Jung and such things, the idea of your shadow reaching through a screen goes hard. quite literally media being able to dig into your psyche directly. I think one of his later movies literally has Freud and Jung in it? I'm very excited to get to that one now.

The discussion of how televisions have become an extension of the physical body One goes back to scanners and how vale connected to a computer system which is just like a human nervous system and Two reaches forward to the way we conceptualize cell phones in the internet today, that our minds have integrated this technology into the way that we think and interface with the world. The unity of man and machine, something that's present even in fast company

Also there are eyes all over this fucking movie. I know it's obvious. But it's still there. We had scanners talk about the eyes actually being doors that allow people in and we have people speaking in this fucking movie and projecting themselves into someone's eyes and that causes the person's worldview to change. It allows the personalities to intermingle and for oblivion to actually spread his message coherently even though he's been dead for so long. One thing I love about Cronenberg is how he will always incorporate paintings and other artwork into the sets that are intended to be examined in conjunction with the movie.

let's talk about metanarrative layers.

The crashes that we see on screen are performed by stunt drivers. There's an actor who plays the role of a stunt driver but the driving was done by a real life stunt driver and not the actor. We have an author named James Ballard who wrote a book where the main character is named James Ballard and he's making a movie. And now in the movie adaptation of this novel, James Ballard is making a movie. We are seeing a movie adapted from a novel about a movie guy making a movie whose name is the same as the author. Goes crazy!

To think about it further, you have a novel where Vaughan stages non-fatal crashes that are recreations of real life fatal crashes. David Cronenberg adapted this into a movie which means he had to stage non-fatal crashes that are recreations of fictional non-fatal crashes that are themselves recreations of Real Life Fatal Crashes. Cronenberg allowed Ballard's vision to live through his movie, his actors and stunt drivers.
